Your first visit

Nervous? Here's exactly what happens

Around one in three of our new patients hadn't seen a dentist in over five years. Nobody here will lecture you.

  • Raise a hand any time and we stop. No questions asked.
  • Noise-cancelling headphones, weighted blankets, and laughing gas available.
  • A written cost estimate before any treatment, always.

  1. Book in two minutes

    Choose your dentist and a time online, or call Hannah at the front desk. We'll text a short health form so there's no clipboard on arrival.

    Today

  2. Meet first, chair second

    Your first 15 minutes are a conversation in our consult room: what's bothering you, what worries you, and what you'd like to get out of the visit.

    Minute 0–15

  3. Exam, photos, and a clear plan

    A gentle exam and clean, then we show you the photos on screen and send a written plan with costs, ordered by urgency.

    Minute 15–60

Questions

Before you book

Can't find your answer? Hannah and Luis on the front desk reply to texts at (207) 555-0142 within the hour.

Do you take new patients?

Yes. We currently have new-patient openings within 7 days for adults and children over 12 months. Emergency patients are seen the same day, whether or not you're registered with us.

How much does a first visit cost without insurance?

A new-patient exam, full X-ray set and hygiene clean is $210 self-pay, or included in the first month of the $29 Harbourline Care Plan. We'll always tell you the price before we start anything.

I'm very anxious about the dentist. What can you do?

Tell us when you book and we'll schedule a longer slot with Dr. Okafor, who leads our nervous-patient program. You can visit just to talk first, with no treatment at all. Nitrous oxide and oral sedation are available.

What counts as a dental emergency?

Severe toothache, a knocked-out or broken tooth, swelling in your face or jaw, or bleeding that won't stop. Call (207) 555-0148. If you have swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, go straight to the Maine Medical Center ER.

What age should my child first see a dentist?

By their first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth. Early visits are short, playful, and mostly about getting comfortable. Siblings can share back-to-back slots.

Can I cancel or reschedule online?

Yes, using the link in your confirmation text. Please give us 24 hours' notice so we can offer the time to someone in pain. There's no fee for your first late cancellation.
